Spoke · market brief · Supabase + Sodabase NYC Hackathon, 4 Nov 2026

Real-time public data is everywhere. Trust in it isn't.

Spoke is a pattern, shown on Citi Bike: ingest a live public feed into Supabase on a schedule, enforce a written data contract, quarantine what fails, let Sodabase learn what normal looks like, and gate every user-facing answer on that state.

The problem, in numbers

Who needs this

  • Riders and trip-planning apps: a wrong "3 bikes" costs a ten-minute walk. Showing "data is 12 min old" is better than a confident wrong number.
  • Operators (Lyft / Citi Bike, other GBFS systems): the quarantine is a ready-made bug list for their own feed: decommissioned docks still published, capacity mismatches.
  • City agencies: NYC DOT reports and plans from this data; a contract and an audit trail make the numbers defensible.
  • Anyone putting an AI assistant on live data: the trust gate generalises. The model is only called when the data has passed its checks.

What exists today

ApproachGap
Consumer apps (Citi Bike app, Google Maps, Transit)Show the feed as-is; no visible freshness or quality signal per station
GBFS validator (MobilityData)Checks schema conformance of a feed, not runtime plausibility (1970 timestamps pass), and doesn't watch over time
Data observability (Soda, Monte Carlo, Great Expectations)Watch the warehouse for engineers; the end user asking a question never sees the verdict
Chat-with-your-data assistantsAnswer fluently from whatever is in the table, stale or not

Why Spoke is different

  • The answer depends on data health. Pipeline metrics and Sodabase's verdict decide whether the model is even called.
  • Contract at the door, twice. TypeScript rules (tested) plus Postgres CHECK constraints.
  • Explainable rejections. Every quarantined row has a reason a human can read and act on.
  • All Supabase. pg_cron, pg_net, Vault, edge functions, RLS: no extra infrastructure to run.

Where it goes next

  • Hour-of-week availability curves per station from status_snapshots ("this dock empties by 8:40 on weekdays").
  • The same contract for every GBFS system (Divvy, Bay Wheels, Capital Bikeshare), configured by feed URL.
  • Quarantine digests sent to the feed operator; Slack alerts via Sodabase for the team.
  • Rolling old snapshots into hourly aggregates (~350,000 rows/day today).

Risks and how we handle them

RiskMitigation
The upstream feed changes shapeUnknown or invalid records are quarantined, not written; a run that rejects over 10% is marked partial and the answer is caveated
The cron chain silently stopsFreshness over 3 minutes makes every answer "untrusted"; Sodabase flags the stale tables
Storage growth on the free tierSnapshots every 10 min, not every minute; roll-ups next
Model invents numbersThe model only phrases a facts block; with no key, a fixed template answers from the same facts
